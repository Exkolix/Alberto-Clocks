Watch Technology

How a watch keeps time

01 / 05

Mechanical Movement

A wound spring supplies the power. A balance wheel keeps the beat.

  1. WindTurning the crown winds the mainspring and stores energy in it.
  2. ReleaseA gear train carries the energy to the escapement, which lets it out in tiny, even steps.
  3. Keep timeThe balance wheel swings back and forth at a steady rate and moves the hands on.

02 / 05

Automatic Winding

A mechanical watch that winds itself as you move.

  1. SwingA weighted rotor turns freely whenever your wrist moves.
  2. WindThrough a small gear train, the rotor winds the mainspring for you.
  3. RunThe movement then works like a mechanical one, with no crown winding needed while you wear it.

03 / 05

Quartz Timekeeping

A battery and a crystal, counting time with great regularity.

  1. PowerA battery sends a small current to a quartz crystal.
  2. VibrateThe crystal vibrates 32,768 times every second, always at the same rate.
  3. CountA circuit counts the vibrations and steps the hands once a second.

04 / 05

Solar Power

Light charges a cell, and the cell runs a quartz movement.

  1. CollectA solar cell under the dial turns light into electricity.
  2. StoreA rechargeable cell holds that power for when it is dark.
  3. RunThe quartz movement draws on the stored power, so it keeps going after dark and rarely needs a new battery.

05 / 05

Smart Sensors

Sensors read your body and your movement, then show the result on the screen.

  1. SenseLight sensors on the case back read your pulse through the skin. An accelerometer and GPS follow your steps and routes.
  2. ProcessA small processor turns the raw readings into clear numbers.
  3. ShareThe watch shows them on its display and sends them to your phone over Bluetooth.
